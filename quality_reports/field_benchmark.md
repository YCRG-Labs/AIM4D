# Field benchmark: AIM4D manuscript vs 10 comparators (2026-07-11)

Method: 4 profiling agents grounded every paper in live Semantic Scholar / publisher
sources (citation counts retrieved 2026-07-11); the manuscript was profiled from full
text under the same critical template. Grades assigned by a single judge on one scale.
Caveats: published papers graded partly from the published record; the manuscript has
full-text advantage and benefits from modern (2026) standards the classics predate.
Impact is citation/uptake-based and not scoreable for an unpublished manuscript, so the
comparable summary column is the at-publication quality average (6 facets, impact excluded).

Key facet notes for the manuscript:
- Rigor 9 (best in set): only paper here that refits its full pipeline per CV fold,
  validates against outcomes coded outside its own measurement tradition, propagates
  the measurement model's uncertainty, and registers falsifiable prospective forecasts.
  Docked from 10 for 46 episodes, V-Dem-derived predictors, predictively inert Stage 4.
- Novelty 6 (near bottom of set): adjudication and architecture, not a new mechanism,
  measure, or framing. Svolik imported a method; L&L coined the field's vocabulary;
  H&B built the measure. This paper orders their hypotheses out of sample.
- Transparency 9: strongest manuscript-stage apparatus in the set, but self-hosted
  registration and zero external contact; ViEWS's 10 reflects years of public,
  prospectively scored operation.
- Impact n/a: the set shows impact tracks novelty-of-framing and timing, not rigor
  (L&L: rigor 5, impact 10; Goldstone: results 5, impact 10). The realistic path to
  impact for this paper is the registered-forecast accountability design, which
  directly answers the known gap in Morgan et al. (live forecasts never scored).
